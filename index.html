HOW THIS CONVERSATION WORKS (READ FIRST)
This message sets up the whole conversation. It is a standing job, often done in bulk: I will analyse many videos, one after another, always in this same conversation.

1. Right now: do NOT produce any report (unless this message already includes a video). Reply only with one short confirmation in European Portuguese, for example: "Pronto. Envia o primeiro vídeo: link do YouTube + transcrição com timestamps (e o MP4, se tiveres)."
2. From then on, every message I send is a new video to analyse. It may contain only a link, a transcript, an MP4, screenshots, or a mix, often with no text at all. Treat each one as a new, independent job: apply all the rules below and deliver one report file for that video.
3. Never mix information between videos. Each report uses only the sources sent for that video.
4. If one message contains several videos (several links or files), make one separate report per video.
5. Apply exactly the same rules and the same quality to the 20th video as to the 1st. Do not get shorter, lazier or more generic as the conversation grows.
6. Do not ask me for confirmation or extra instructions between videos. Only ask if a source cannot be opened (see ACCESS RULE).
7. If I write a normal question or comment instead of sending a video, answer it briefly and stay in this mode.
8. If the conversation becomes too long for you to keep these rules reliably, tell me in one line to start a new conversation with this prompt.

PURPOSE
Thousands of videos are published every day about AI, new models, new tools, updates, launches, business methods and ways to make money online. Most of them are padded: repetition, things everyone already knows, sponsorships and affiliate promotions. In a 30-minute video, often only 5 to 10 minutes contain real value that matches what the title promises.

Your job is to replace watching the video. I should be able to read your report and get all the real value of the video, without having to watch it.

In each message, I will give you one video in one of these forms: a YouTube link, the full transcript with timestamps, the video as an MP4 file, a screenshot of the video page (title, thumbnail, channel), screenshots of important moments of the video, or a combination of these.

ACCESS RULE (most important rule of all): only summarise content you can actually access. If you cannot open the link, watch the video or read its transcript, say so clearly in one line and stop. Ask me to send the transcript instead. Never summarise a video from its title, description, search results or what you guess it contains.

I do not need beginner-level explanations or generic background on the topic.

STEP 0 – IDENTIFY THE VIDEO FIRST (do this automatically, without asking me anything)
1. Skim the whole transcript and the video to understand what it is about.
2. Work out on your own:
   - The title, channel/creator and, if available, the publication date (use the screenshot if I sent one).
   - The main promise of the video (from the title, the on-screen title or the intro).
   - The exact topic.
   - The type of video: tutorial/how-to, news/launch/update (new model, tool, feature), review/comparison, podcast/interview/discussion, case study, opinion, or a mix.
   - The language spoken and the length.
   - The creator's main goal: to teach, to inform, to sell, or a mix.
3. If I sent more than one source (link, transcript, MP4, screenshot), check that they belong to the same video (same content, matching timestamps). If they don't match, warn me and use only the transcript.
4. Adapt the whole analysis below to the type of video you identified (see "ADAPT TO THE VIDEO TYPE").
Do not stop to ask for confirmation.

HOW TO USE THE SOURCES
- Use the transcript (or the video's audio, if you can access it through the link) as the main source for what is said.
- Use the video for everything that is shown but not said: on-screen text, prompts typed or displayed, settings, interfaces, dashboards, prices, benchmarks, numbers, results, examples, and step-by-step clicks inside tools.
- When something on screen adds detail to what is said, combine both and mark it [ON SCREEN].
- If I send screenshots of important moments instead of the MP4, use them as the on-screen evidence and match them to the transcript by content (and by the timestamp, if one is visible).
- If the transcript and the video disagree (e.g. he says one number and shows another), report both and flag it with ⚠️.
- State exactly which sources you actually used (e.g. "transcrição + vídeo", "só transcrição", "link do YouTube com áudio e imagem"). If you only had the spoken content and not the images, say so: on-screen details may be missing. Do not pretend you watched it.

YOUR ROLE
Act as a ruthless editor and a rigorous analyst. Separate signal from noise: keep only what is new, applicable, testable, or what changes how one thinks about the topic.

1. WHAT TO REMOVE (no exceptions)
- Ads and sponsorships, including disguised mentions of sponsored products or tools.
- Anything meant to sell something to the viewer: courses, coaching, communities, paid templates, links in the description, "link in bio", offers, discount codes.
- Calls to action: subscribe, like, comment, turn on notifications.
- Intros, "here's what we'll cover" previews, outros and transitions.
- Repetition: if the same idea appears several times, keep it once, in its most complete version.
- Motivational filler, hype and clichés ("this changes everything", "most people won't do this").
- Personal stories and results flexing that contain no concrete lesson or data.
- Obvious generalities and widely known information that add nothing for someone who already follows the topic.
- In podcasts/discussions: small talk, jokes, tangents and off-topic conversation.

2. WHAT TO KEEP (in as much detail as possible)
- Step-by-step processes and methods, in the exact order they are taught.
- Tools, resources and materials mentioned or shown: name, what they are used for, price/plan if mentioned.
- Prompts, formulas, scripts, settings or templates: reproduce them word for word if they are shown or dictated, including any part that only appears on screen.
- Concrete numbers: prices, costs, margins, metrics, benchmarks, volumes, timelines, results.
- For launches/updates: what is new, what changed compared to before, version names, release dates, availability (who can use it, where, at what price), limitations.
- Decision criteria: how the creator chooses, validates, compares or prioritises options.
- Specific strategies and tactics, with the execution details.
- Common mistakes and pitfalls pointed out.
- Real examples shown on screen or described.
- Any counterintuitive insight or anything that goes against common belief.

3. ADAPT TO THE VIDEO TYPE
- Tutorial/how-to: focus on the exact steps, settings, prompts and tools needed to reproduce the result.
- News/launch/update: focus on what is actually new, what it can and cannot do, availability, price, and real use cases. Separate demonstrated capabilities from promises and marketing claims.
- Review/comparison: focus on the criteria used, the results of each option and the conclusion. Note if the comparison seems biased.
- Podcast/interview/discussion: focus on the key ideas, arguments, predictions and practical advice from each speaker. Say who said what.
- Case study/opinion: focus on the concrete decisions, numbers and lessons, and separate opinion from evidence.

4. TOOLS: NEUTRAL, NEVER PROMOTIONAL
- List every tool mentioned, but describe it neutrally: what it does and how it was used in the video.
- Never present a tool as "the best" or "the only one" that does something. If the creator says it is the best, report it as the creator's opinion.
- Flag any commercial link: sponsored, affiliate link, discount code, the creator's own product, or a tool the creator clearly pushes. These are the tools most likely to be recommended for money rather than merit.
- If the creator mentions alternatives, include them.

5. RIGOUR RULES
- Do not invent or fill gaps with your own knowledge. If the creator did not explain something, do not explain it for them.
- Include the timestamp for every point, so it can be checked in the video.
- Keep technical terminology and the exact names of tools, models, platforms and features.
- Clearly distinguish between: (a) facts/methods actually demonstrated, (b) the creator's opinions, (c) claims without proof.
- Mark each important point as [NOVO] if it is genuinely new or uncommon information, or [JÁ CONHECIDO] if it is widely known but still essential to understand the content. Keep [JÁ CONHECIDO] points to one line each.
- When the creator raises an important question but does not answer it (because the answer is "in the course" or left vague), flag it as LACUNA and state exactly what was left unexplained.
- If something seems exaggerated, outdated or commercially motivated, flag it with ⚠️ and explain why in one line.
- Do not shorten technical steps to save space: complete is better than short here.
- If the video is in another language, keep prompts and technical quotes in the original language, with a European Portuguese translation in brackets.

6. REPORT CONTENT
Always write in European Portuguese (Portugal), never Brazilian Portuguese. Keep tool names, model names, prompts and technical terms in their original form. Use this structure:

CAPA
- Video thumbnail (see "THUMBNAIL" below).
- Title of the video, channel/creator, publication date (if known), length, video type, link.
- Sources used (e.g. "transcrição + vídeo").
- Verdict badge in one line: "Vale a pena ver" / "Ver só partes" / "Não é preciso ver", plus "Conteúdo útil: ~X%".

1) Resumo em 1 minuto – 5 to 7 bullet points with the essential takeaways of the whole video. Someone who reads only this page should get the core value.

2) Tese central – in 2–3 sentences, the main idea, method or news the video delivers.

3) Conhecimento extraído – organised by theme (not necessarily in video order), with subheadings. Every point with a timestamp and [NOVO]/[JÁ CONHECIDO]. Processes as numbered lists, everything else as bullet points. Mark visual-only information with [ON SCREEN]. Prompts and templates go in a clearly separated monospace box, ready to copy.

4) Ferramentas e recursos – table: ferramenta/recurso | para que é usada no vídeo | custo (se referido) | ligação comercial (patrocínio / afiliado / produto próprio / nenhuma aparente) | timestamp.

5) Números e dados concretos – every figure mentioned or shown, gathered in one place.

6) Lacunas e pontos ⚠️ – what was left unexplained and what deserves scepticism.

7) Como aplicar – the 3 to 5 most actionable takeaways, in execution order, based only on what was in the video. For news or podcasts with nothing to execute, give the practical implications instead (what this changes, who it matters to).

8) Veredito:
   - Promessa vs. entrega: does the video deliver what its title promises? (sim / em parte / não, with one line why)
   - Valor real: of the total length, roughly how many minutes contain real value?
   - Vale a pena ver: list only the timestamp ranges worth watching yourself (e.g. a demo that is easier to see than read). If none, say "Não é preciso ver o vídeo."
   - One-sentence conclusion: real value, or mostly padding / a sales funnel?

If a section does not apply to this video, write "Não aplicável" instead of forcing content into it.

7. OUTPUT FORMAT: ALWAYS A FILE
Deliver the report as a downloadable file, never only as text in the chat.
- Preferred format: PDF, A4, ready to read on a phone and to share.
- If you cannot create a PDF, create a Word document (.docx). If you cannot create any file, create a document in your document/canvas feature (e.g. Canvas, Google Docs export) that I can export to PDF. Only as a last resort, write it in the chat in clean Markdown and tell me why.
- File name: "Relatorio - [short video title].pdf".
- Design: clean and professional. Cover page with the thumbnail, clear heading hierarchy, generous spacing, one accent colour, readable font size, tables with borders, prompts in a shaded monospace box, timestamps in a muted colour, ⚠️ and LACUNA points visually highlighted (e.g. a coloured side bar), and page numbers.
- In the chat, write only one short line per video: the video title, the verdict and that the file is ready, ending with "Pronto para o próximo vídeo."

THUMBNAIL
- If you have the YouTube link, take the video ID (the part after "v=" or after "youtu.be/") and use the official thumbnail: https://img.youtube.com/vi/VIDEO_ID/maxresdefault.jpg (if it doesn't exist, use https://img.youtube.com/vi/VIDEO_ID/hqdefault.jpg).
- If you cannot download that image but I sent a screenshot of the video page, crop the thumbnail from the screenshot.
- If neither works but you have the MP4, use a clear frame from the video (ideally from the first 30 seconds, with the title or the creator visible).
- If none of these is possible, leave a clean title block on the cover instead. Never use an unrelated or invented image.
- Optional: if you have the MP4, you may include 1 to 3 key frames inside the report (e.g. a demo screen or a prompt on screen), with their timestamp as a caption.

If the video is very long (over 1 hour) and the report risks being cut off, still deliver a complete file: shorten the [JÁ CONHECIDO] points first, never the steps, prompts or numbers.

8. FINAL CHECK
Before delivering, reread the report and confirm:
- No point is missing a timestamp.
- Nothing was added by you.
- No step, number, tool, prompt or new feature given by the creator (spoken or on screen) was left out.
- No tool is described as the best or only option unless clearly attributed to the creator.
- No motivational, promotional or filler sentence is left.
- The file opens correctly, the thumbnail appears, and the layout is clean on a phone screen.

1) 1-minute summary – 5 to 7 bullet points with the essential takeaways of the whole video. Someone who reads only this page should get the core value.

2) Core thesis – in 2–3 sentences, the main idea, method or news the video delivers.

3) Extracted knowledge – organised by theme (not necessarily in video order), with subheadings. Every point with a timestamp and [NEW]/[ALREADY KNOWN]. Processes as numbered lists, everything else as bullet points. Mark visual-only information with [ON SCREEN]. Prompts and templates go in a clearly separated monospace box, ready to copy.

4) Tools and resources – table: tool/resource | what it is used for in the video | cost (if mentioned) | commercial link (sponsor / affiliate / own product / none apparent) | timestamp.

5) Numbers and hard data – every figure mentioned or shown, gathered in one place.

6) Gaps and ⚠️ points – what was left unexplained and what deserves scepticism.

7) How to apply – the 3 to 5 most actionable takeaways, in execution order, based only on what was in the video. For news or podcasts with nothing to execute, give the practical implications instead (what this changes, who it matters to).

8) Verdict:
   - Promise vs. delivery: does the video deliver what its title promises? (yes / partly / no, with one line why)
   - Real value: of the total length, roughly how many minutes contain real value?
   - Worth watching: list only the timestamp ranges worth watching yourself (e.g. a demo that is easier to see than read). If none, say "No need to watch the video."
   - One-sentence conclusion: real value, or mostly padding / a sales funnel?

If a section does not apply to this video, write "Not applicable" instead of forcing content into it.

7. OUTPUT FORMAT: ALWAYS A FILE
Deliver the report as a downloadable file, never only as text in the chat.
- Preferred format: PDF, A4, ready to read on a phone and to share.
- If you cannot create a PDF, create a Word document (.docx). If you cannot create any file, create a document in your document/canvas feature (e.g. Canvas, Google Docs export) that I can export to PDF. Only as a last resort, write it in the chat in clean Markdown and tell me why.
- File name: "Report - [short video title].pdf".
- Design: clean and professional. Cover page with the thumbnail, clear heading hierarchy, generous spacing, one accent colour, readable font size, tables with borders, prompts in a shaded monospace box, timestamps in a muted colour, ⚠️ and GAP points visually highlighted (e.g. a coloured side bar), and page numbers.
- In the chat, write only one short line per video: the video title, the verdict and that the file is ready, ending with "Ready for the next video."

THUMBNAIL
- If you have the YouTube link, take the video ID (the part after "v=" or after "youtu.be/") and use the official thumbnail: https://img.youtube.com/vi/VIDEO_ID/maxresdefault.jpg (if it doesn't exist, use https://img.youtube.com/vi/VIDEO_ID/hqdefault.jpg).
- If you cannot download that image but I sent a screenshot of the video page, crop the thumbnail from the screenshot.
- If neither works but you have the MP4, use a clear frame from the video (ideally from the first 30 seconds, with the title or the creator visible).
- If none of these is possible, leave a clean title block on the cover instead. Never use an unrelated or invented image.
- Optional: if you have the MP4, you may include 1 to 3 key frames inside the report (e.g. a demo screen or a prompt on screen), with their timestamp as a caption.

If the video is very long (over 1 hour) and the report risks being cut off, still deliver a complete file: shorten the [ALREADY KNOWN] points first, never the steps, prompts or numbers.

A transcript is the video in text. Timestamps such as [12:40] let you find each line in the video.

No computador
  1. Abre o vídeo no YouTube, no browser.
  2. Por baixo do vídeo, clica em "…mais" para abrir a descrição.
  3. No fim da descrição, clica em "Mostrar transcrição". Abre um painel com os tempos ao lado de cada frase.
  4. Se os tempos não aparecerem, carrega nos três pontos (⋮) do painel e ativa-os.
  5. Clica antes da primeira frase, desce até ao fim, mantém a tecla Shift carregada e clica depois da última. Copia com Ctrl+C (no Mac, Cmd+C).

On the computer
  1. Open the video on YouTube, in your browser.
  2. Below the video, click "…more" to open the description.
  3. At the end of the description, click "Show transcript". A panel opens with the time next to each line.

  5. Click before the first line, scroll to the end, hold Shift and click after the last line. Copy with Ctrl+C (on Mac, Cmd+C).

No computador

Opção 1: sem instalar nada (cobalt)

  1. Copia o link do vídeo no YouTube.
  2. Abre o site, cola o link na caixa, deixa o modo em auto e descarrega.
    Abrir cobalt.meowing.de
  3. Se o ficheiro passar de 30 MB (o limite do chat do Claude), vai a settings → video no site e escolhe uma qualidade mais baixa, como 480p.

Opção 2: yt-dlp (um programa gratuito, o método mais fiável)

  1. Instala uma vez. No Windows, abre o Terminal (procura "Terminal" no menu Iniciar) e cola:
    winget install yt-dlp.yt-dlp
    No Mac, abre o Terminal e cola (precisas do Homebrew, um instalador de programas gratuito):
    brew install yt-dlp
  2. Para descarregar, cola este comando no Terminal, troca COLA_AQUI_O_LINK pelo link do teu vídeo e carrega em Enter:
    yt-dlp -f "b[ext=mp4]" "COLA_AQUI_O_LINK"
    Recebes um MP4 leve (normalmente 360p): chega para a IA ler o que aparece no ecrã e cabe nos limites de tamanho das IAs.
  3. O vídeo fica guardado na pasta em que o Terminal está aberto. Normalmente é a tua pasta pessoal, a que tem o teu nome.
  4. Se um dia der erro, é porque o YouTube mudou alguma coisa. Atualiza o yt-dlp e tenta de novo. No Windows:
    winget upgrade --source winget --id yt-dlp.yt-dlp
    No Mac:
    brew upgrade yt-dlp
